Data City · game design · v1

Every naira you spend, you earned with data.

Get hired, find the story in the numbers, and climb from a buka's receipt book to a boardroom. The city is Lagos-flavoured and fictional; the analysis is real SQL, graded on hidden data. Money buys attire, cars and eventually aircraft, and the streets talk back.

Time of day

One in-game day takes about 24 real minutes. Offices are open 9–5, bukas peak at lunch, night markets open in the evening. Some gigs appear only in their window.

Every job has five beats: brief, field evidence, workstation, present findings, consequence. The server runs your query on hidden fixtures and compares results, so any correct query passes and keyword tricks fail.

How a score becomes pay

Weights: correctness 50%, trap fixture 20%, findings 20%, independence 10%. Below 60% is a fail: you get a small call-out fee and can retry. The server grants pay once per attempt.

In-game naira is fictional and only graded analysis pays meaningfully. Reputation is tracked per employer and per district. Ranks run Cadet → Junior Analyst → Analyst → Senior → Lead → Principal → Partner.

Four ways to get a car

  • Hail a taxi or danfo and pay the fare to skip ahead.
  • Borrow from a contact with trust ≥ 60 for a day. Damage or a late return costs trust and money.
  • Rent by the hour.
  • Buy at the dealership; it lives in your garage.

Attire opens doors

  • Tier 4 front desks expect corporate attire.
  • Traditional attire earns bonuses with family firms and at events.
  • Well-dressed players get compliments on the street.

You

  • Quick-pick from six preset cadets, or customise body, skin tone, hair and outfit.
  • Built on the shared-skeleton characters already in the game, so every animation fits every look.
  • Wardrobe slots: top, bottom, dress, shoes, headwear, accessory, colour, pattern.
  • Fabric patterns now (ankara, aso-oke, pinstripe); authored outfit meshes such as wide-sleeved agbada come later.

Movement

  • Walk, jog, sprint; turn in place; jump; a hop up kerbs.
  • Clip speed matches movement speed, so feet don't slide.
  • Sit and type at workstations; gestures while talking; drive.
  • NPCs turn their heads to look at you and vary their idles.

Cars queued behind also honk, staggered and quieter. After a long jam a danfo may mount the kerb and go round. There are no injuries; bumping people or cars costs reputation and a small "settle it" fine.

Pedestrians

  • Bumped: a short shout.
  • Sprint through a crowd: people sidestep and comment.
  • A loud argument nearby: onlookers gather, then disperse.
  • High district reputation: greetings by name.

Driving

  • Rapier ray-cast vehicle physics with arcade tuning and a chase camera.
  • Traffic follows a lane graph with junctions and traffic lights; cars look ahead for you and each other.
  • Danfos stop at bus stops while conductors call the route.

Flying (late game)

  • Airfield district; earn the licence at flight school.
  • Chopper (hover model) and light plane (lift and drag).
  • Helipads on the bank roof and in Tech Park; aerial survey gigs give Tier 5 cases new data.
Guardrails. No weapons or violence, no gambling or real-money purchases. Every company, brand and character is fictional. Money and inventory are decided by the server, never the client.

Each phase ends playable. Engineering tasks live in the roadmap; this is the order in which the game takes shape.
